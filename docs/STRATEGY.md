# Growth Strategy

This is the thinking the engine runs on. Edit `config/content_strategy.yaml` to change any
of it — this doc explains *why* each choice was made.

## Positioning

**You:** an independent builder shipping **AI receptionists** — custom AI assistants that
answer a business's customers instantly, 24/7, qualify them, and book them in.

**Angle:** you don't *talk about* AI, you *ship it in public*. Every build becomes content.
The content pipeline itself (this repo) is proof of what you sell.

**Why this offer:** it's hot (easy reach), extremely demo-able (each build is a clip/screenshot),
and it fits your skills (websites + Python automation + AI). You can broaden later; a sharp
offer now makes content, DMs, and niche fall into place.

**Market:** English primary (reach), Dutch variants for local NL businesses (low competition,
high trust). Start with local service SMBs (clinics, salons, trades, agencies) — they feel the
"missed call = missed money" pain hardest — but the content also lands with online businesses.

## The one-line pitch

> "Your business gets an AI that answers every customer instantly, 24/7, and books them in —
> so you stop losing leads to slow replies."

## Content pillars (rotated automatically)

The engine rotates through four pillars so aggressive volume (2–3/day) stays varied and
sustainable. Each maps to a distinct job:

| Pillar | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Build log** | Show momentum, build trust | "Day 3 of building an AI receptionist for a dental clinic. Today: handling reschedules without a human. Here's the tricky part…" |
| **Teardown** | Create demand | "This barbershop misses ~15 calls a week. That's ~€600 in walk-ins gone. Here's the 3-step fix." |
| **Proof / demo** | Convert | A clip/screenshot of the bot booking an appointment, with a one-line caption. |
| **Hot take / tip** | Reach + personality | "Most 'AI chatbots' for small business fail for one boring reason: nobody wired them to the calendar." |

## Cadence

- Target: **aggressive (2–3/day)** across X + LinkedIn, lighter on Reddit.
- Reality check: aggressive volume is only good if quality holds. The engine ramps — start at
  the "starter" cadence in the config for a week, confirm the drafts are strong, then raise it.
- Consistency beats bursts. Better 2 good posts every day than 6 on Monday and silence.

## Platform rules (why the split)

- **X:** has a usable write API → auto-posts on your approval.
- **LinkedIn:** posting API is gated and automation is against ToS → the engine formats the
  post and pushes it to you to paste. LinkedIn rewards native, personal posts anyway.
- **Reddit:** value-first only. The engine drafts a genuinely helpful comment/post for a
  relevant subreddit; you post it manually. Obvious automation gets you banned fast. Treat
  Reddit as 1–2 high-effort value posts a week, not volume.

## 30-day plan

- **Week 1 — Wire it up & warm up.** Get the engine running, approve at "starter" cadence.
  Ship your first 3 build logs from a demo AI receptionist you build for a fictional/local
  business. Optimize your X + LinkedIn bios to the pitch above.
- **Week 2 — Find the angle that lands.** Raise to ~1/day. Post one teardown of a real local
  business type each day. Note which posts get replies/saves — that's your demand signal.
- **Week 3 — Add proof.** Ship a working demo (even for a fake business) and post the clip.
  Start soft outreach: reply helpfully in 2 relevant subreddits/LinkedIn threads a day.
- **Week 4 — Scale what works.** Go to 2–3/day on the pillars that pulled. First DMs → offer a
  free/cheap pilot AI receptionist to 1–2 local businesses to get a real case study.

## The DM → client path (manual, for now)

Content's job is to start conversations. When someone engages:
1. Reply with genuine value, no pitch.
2. If they're a fit, move to DM: "Want me to show you what this would look like for [their
   business]? I'll build a quick demo, free."
3. Deliver a small demo → that's your proof and your close.

The engine gets the numbers up and the conversations started. Closing is you — for now. Once
you have volume, a lightweight DM-triage assistant is the natural next automation to build
(and sell).
